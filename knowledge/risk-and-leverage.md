# Risk, leverage and margin

## What is leverage?
Leverage lets you control a larger position with a smaller deposit. For example, with 1:30 leverage, $1,000 of margin controls a $30,000 position. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses. A small price move against you can cause a large loss compared with your deposit.

## What is margin?
Margin is the amount of money needed to open and keep a leveraged position. It is a deposit set aside from your balance, not a fee. Free margin is the money left for new trades or to absorb losses.

## What is a margin call and a stop-out?
A margin call is a warning that your account equity is falling close to the margin needed to keep positions open. If losses continue and the margin level falls below the stop-out level, the platform may automatically close positions to limit further losses.

## What is a stop-loss?
A stop-loss is an order that closes your trade if the price moves against you to a level you choose. It helps limit losses. A standard stop-loss does not guarantee execution at the exact price: in fast markets or price gaps it can be filled at a worse price (slippage).

## What is a take-profit?
A take-profit order closes your trade automatically when the price reaches a target level, locking in the gain.

## What is slippage?
Slippage is the difference between the price you expected and the price your order was actually filled at. It happens in fast-moving or illiquid markets, and around major news.

## What is a price gap?
A gap is when the price jumps from one level to another with no trading in between, often over a weekend or after major news. Stop orders can be filled at the next available price, which may be far from the level you set.

## What is volatility?
Volatility describes how much and how quickly a price moves. Higher volatility means bigger and faster price swings, which raises both opportunity and risk.

## How can I manage trading risk?
Common risk-management practices:
- Only trade money you can afford to lose.
- Risk a small percentage of your account per trade. For example, many traders risk no more than 1–2%; 1% of a $5,000 account is $50.
- Use stop-loss orders and know where you will exit before you enter.
- Use lower leverage and smaller position sizes while learning.
- Avoid trading on emotion and avoid chasing losses.
- Practise first and understand the costs (spread, commission, swaps).

## What is position sizing?
Position sizing is choosing how big a trade to open so that, if your stop-loss is hit, you only lose the amount you planned to risk. It depends on your account size, the risk percentage and the distance to your stop-loss.

## What is risk–reward ratio?
The risk–reward ratio compares how much you might lose (to your stop-loss) with how much you aim to gain (to your take-profit). For example, risking 20 pips to target 40 pips is a 1:2 ratio.
