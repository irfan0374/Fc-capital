# Trading basics

## What is forex trading?
Forex (foreign exchange) trading is buying one currency while selling another. Currencies are always quoted in pairs, such as EUR/USD. Prices move with economic data, interest rates, central bank decisions, politics and market sentiment.

## What is a currency pair? What are the base and quote currencies?
A currency pair shows the price of one currency in another. In EUR/USD, the euro is the base currency (first) and the US dollar is the quote currency (second). If EUR/USD is 1.1000, one euro is worth 1.10 US dollars.

## What are major currency pairs?
Major pairs include the US dollar and another large economy's currency, for example EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD and USD/CAD. They are usually the most traded and liquid pairs, often with tighter spreads.

## What is a pip?
A pip is a standard unit of price movement in forex. For most pairs it is the fourth decimal place (0.0001). For pairs quoted in Japanese yen it is the second decimal place (0.01). For example, EUR/USD moving from 1.1000 to 1.1005 is a 5-pip move.

## What is a lot?
A lot is the size of a trade. In forex, one standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency, a mini lot is 10,000 and a micro lot is 1,000. Bigger lots mean each pip move is worth more money, so both profit and loss are larger.

## What is the spread?
The spread is the difference between the bid (sell) price and the ask (buy) price. It is a trading cost: when you open a trade you start slightly negative by the size of the spread. Spreads can widen in fast or illiquid markets, for example around major news.

## What is a commission?
A commission is a separate fee charged per trade, usually per lot. Commission-based accounts (like a raw account) usually have tighter spreads plus a commission, while spread-based accounts (like a standard account) usually include the cost in the spread.

## What does going long or short mean?
Going long (buy) means you expect the price to rise. A long position gains when the price goes up. Going short (sell) means you expect the price to fall. A short position gains when the price goes down. Costs like spread and commission still apply.

## What is a CFD?
A CFD (contract for difference) is an agreement to exchange the difference in an asset's price between when the trade is opened and when it is closed. You don't own the underlying asset. CFDs let you trade rising or falling prices, and they are usually traded with leverage, which increases risk. CFDs are complex instruments and carry a high risk of losing money rapidly.

## What is an index CFD?
A stock index measures the performance of a group of companies, for example a US large-cap index (US 500), a US technology index (US Tech) or a UK large-cap index (UK 100). An index CFD lets you trade the index price movements without buying the individual shares.

## What are commodities like gold and oil?
Commodities are raw materials. Gold and silver are metals, often watched as "safe havens" in uncertain times. Crude oil is an energy commodity affected by supply, demand, production decisions and global events. Commodity CFDs let you trade their price moves without owning the physical goods.

## What is a swap or overnight fee?
When a leveraged position stays open overnight, a swap (rollover) fee or credit may apply. It reflects the interest-rate difference between the two currencies or the cost of financing the position. Swaps can be positive or negative and can add up over time.

## What moves currency and market prices?
Common drivers include interest rate decisions, inflation, employment data, GDP, central bank comments, politics, global risk sentiment and unexpected news. Prices can move very quickly around major announcements.

## What is a trading session?
The forex market trades 24 hours a day, five days a week, through the Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York sessions. Activity and volatility are usually highest when the London and New York sessions overlap.
